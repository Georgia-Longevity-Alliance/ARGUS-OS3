## The mechanism in one sentence

> **The organism ages because the oldest centrioles — carrying the most errors — accumulate in stem cells: division tempo falls and niche-signal perception falls (centrosome + cilium function), so tissue self-repair declines while self-destruction rises — imbalance: repair↓ / destruction↑.**

## The chain (5 links)

1. **Retention [E]** — In asymmetric stem-cell divisions, the older (mother) centrosome is inherited by the cell that keeps stemness (Yamashita 2007, PMID 17255513; Wang 2009, PMID 19829375). Progeny do **not** eliminate centrioles (unlike C. elegans, where 88% of somatic cells eliminate; planarian neoblasts lack centrioles). The ratchet is never released in the somatic stem-cell lineage.

2. **Temporal entropy accumulation [H]** — Templated geometry change (mother→daughter, semi-conservative duplication; Kochanski & Borisy 1990, PMID 2335566) is **hypothesized to be PROGRAMMED — possibly a part of the differentiation mechanism itself** (H_programmed, falsifiable via cryo-ET comparison of de novo vs. templated geometry), and is inseparable from superimposed stochastic errors (β·t + η(t), not decomposable in vivo). Together they form the **temporal entropy** of the retained oldest centriole, which rises monotonically in daughter stem cells.
   - **v8.1 clarification ( "contradiction 2"):** "programmed" and "stochastic" are NOT contradictory for the same parameter — they are **two additive components acting at different levels**: β·t is a deterministic, systematic trend component (templated geometry drift, reproducible across cells), while η(t) is the zero-mean stochastic fluctuation around it (cell-to-cell noise). This is the standard signal-plus-noise decomposition; the two are separable only in principle (in vivo they convolve, hence the cryo-ET falsification strategy compares de novo vs. templated geometry *distributions*, not single cells).

3. **Functional decline [I]** — The oldest centriole impairs two functions:
   - **Centrosome** → division tempo ↓ (defective spindle/duplication)
   - **Cilium** → niche-signal perception ↓ (antenna for Hedgehog/Wnt/PDGFRα)

4. **Tissue imbalance [H]** — Division tempo ↓ + niche responses ↓ → **tissue self-repair ↓**, while **self-destruction ↑** (apoptosis, senescence, degradation). Aging is the imbalance itself — not a "breakdown," not a teleological "price."

5. **Outcome** — Regeneration cannot keep up with destruction → stem-cell exhaustion, division limit, organismal aging.

## The germline contrast

The germline is the only compartment that **releases the ratchet**: it eliminates centrioles (meiosis) and rebuilds de novo (blastomeres), clearing programmed geometry AND stochastic damage **in one act** — the existence proof that multi-counter entropy reversal is biologically possible. The **Entropy Reset Protocol** reproduces this in somatic cells **without the meiotic price** (diploidy preserved, no meiotic recombination).

## 1. Focused Research Question

> **[P] Can experimentally induced centriole elimination followed by verified de novo centriole biogenesis produce measurable, OSK-independent improvements in defined cellular aging phenotypes, while preserving genomic integrity?**

This is deliberately narrower than "reset aging" — it is testable in a dish, pre-registrable, and falsifiable. **Explicit extrapolation boundary (per earlier review):** Phase 1 uses untransformed fibroblasts, which do **not** undergo asymmetric centrosome inheritance — Phase 1 tests *feasibility of the reset* (H1) and the cellular-aging phenotype (H2), **not** the stem-cell retention mechanism itself (which requires Phase 3 systems with demonstrated asymmetric inheritance, e.g., organoids or in vivo models). ## 2. What Is Established [E]

### 2.1 Programmed centriole elimination is real
- Kalbfuss & Gönczy (2023, *Open Biology*, PMID 37963546): review of centriole elimination pathways.
- Kalbfuss & Gönczy (2023, *Science Advances*, PMID 37256957): **primary work** — 88% of C. elegans embryonic cells eliminate centrioles on a stereotyped schedule.

### 2.2 De novo centriole biogenesis is real
- Khodjakov et al. (2002, PMID 12356862): vertebrate cells assemble centrosomes de novo after removal.
- Takumi & Kitagawa (2022, *Front Cell Dev Biol*, PMID 35445021): experimental and natural induction of de novo centriole formation.
- Gönczy (2026, *Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol*, PMID 41310006): canonical biogenesis pathway (PLK4–STIL–SAS-6).
- **Aggregate-seeded de novo model (v7.4):** CEP152–CEP63–PCNT aggregates propagate outside the centriole and seed de novo biogenesis (Ozaki et al., 2025, *bioRxiv*, PMID 40667363, preprint — disclosed as such) — a molecular description of the template-independent route this protocol must prove.
- **Caveat [E]:** in most proliferating cells, the dominant route is canonical (next to the existing centriole); the de novo pathway is a specialized state — the protocol must prove it was used (see §7.3).
- **Centriole stability [E, v7.4]:** mechanisms underlying centriole stability are now systematically reviewed (Biven & Wang 2025, *J Biol Chem*, PMID 41167311) — the molecular basis for the "templated geometry change" claim (H_programmed) and its falsifiable cryo-ET test (§4.3).

### 2.2a Centriolar proteins and cellular senescence [E→I, v7.4]
- **POC5 regulates human adipogenesis and cellular senescence** (Pistorio et al., 2026, *FASEB J*, PMID 42507085) — a centriolar protein directly modulates a senescence-associated phenotype in primary human cells; independent support (beyond Robichaud 2024) for the centriole–senescence connection at the protein level.

### 2.2b The scaffold-stability argument ( rebuttal)
> Reviewer v11: "The centriole is a scaffold, not a hard disk; centrosomal proteins turn over rapidly, so PTM errors cannot accumulate."

**Rebuttal (with literature):** (1) the centriole is a **semi-stable, long-lived organelle** — its structural proteins (notably centriolar tubulin) are not in rapid exchange with the cytoplasmic pool; the mechanisms maintaining this stability are now systematically reviewed (Biven & Wang 2025, *J Biol Chem*, PMID 41167311 — "Mechanisms underlying centriole stability"); (2) the tubulin PTM code (polyglutamylation, detyrosination, Δ2-tubulin) is deposited **processively and irreversibly** on assembled microtubule arrays — the relevant enzymes (TTLL5/6, CCP5) act on polymerized tubulin, and Δ2-tubulin is a terminal, non-removable modification (recent code reviews: J Biomed Sci 2026, PMID 42083040; Adv Sci 2025, PMID 40433930); (3) the centriole therefore accumulates PTM entropy **because** its subunits are retained — this is precisely the CEDAR premise, now anchored in the stability literature. Empirical resolution is pre-registered: PTM mass spectrometry on aged vs. de novo centrioles (Proof D, §6.3) directly measures whether accumulation occurs on the retained scaffold.

### 2.3 Centriole fate at fertilization is taxon-specific [E]
- Developmental and tissue-specific roles of mammalian centrosomes are now comprehensively reviewed (Meyer-Gerards & Bazzi 2025, *FEBS J*, PMID 38935637) — consolidates the [E] claims in §2.3–2.4 and the tissue-specificity caveat (Januschke 2011 neuroblast counterexample).
- Mouse oocytes lose centrioles during oogenesis (Simerly 2018, PMID 30143724; Schatten 2005, PMID 15385423).
- Starfish eliminate mother and daughter centrioles by distinct mechanisms (Borrego-Pinto 2016, PMID 27002173); Drosophila via Polo/ANA1 (Pimenta-Marques 2016, PMID 27229142).
- Avidor-Reiss & Fishman (2019, *Reproduction*, PMID 30496124): zygotes obtain two centrioles by diverse routes — paternal structures, maternal retention, or de novo.
- **Correction of earlier wording [v3.0→v4.0]:** there is **no universal "sperm centriole = seed, not a template."** A defensible statement is: *a paternal centriole or centriole-derived structure can provide a platform for subsequent centriole assembly in some taxa, whereas other metazoan systems rely substantially on de novo centriole formation.*

### 2.4 Asymmetric centrosome inheritance biases fate [E]
- Yamashita et al. (2007, *Science*, PMID 17255513); Wang et al. (2009, *Nature*, PMID 19829375); Januschke et al. (2011, PMID 21407209); Barandun et al. (2025, PMID 39764850); Zhao et al. (2025, PMID 41315244); Chen & Yamashita (2021, PMID 33435817).
- **Centrosome heterogeneity in stem cells regulates cell diversity (v7.6):** Camargo Ortega & Götz (2022, *Trends Cell Biol*, PMID 35750615) — consensus review on centrosome heterogeneity in stem cells; Bolkent (2024, *Genes to Cells*, PMID 39379096) — mechanisms of asymmetric stem-cell division in tissue homeostasis. Both strengthen [E] status of the inheritance claim; an independent assessment of "centrosomes regulate fate via MT organization and signaling (Hedgehog/Wnt)" is consistent with, not contradictory to, CEDAR (cilium-mediated route H3/H8).
- **Cilium–aging axis (v7.5):** primary cilia shape the hallmarks of health and aging (Trends Mol Med review, 2023, PMID 37137787); a primary-cilia–autophagy axis in hippocampal neurons is essential to maintain cognitive resilience in aging (Nature Aging, 2025, PMID 39984747 — note: 2025, not 2024 as previously cited). Since the primary cilium is templated on the mother centriole, these findings independently strengthen the cilium-mediated route (H3, H8) — the cilium is the functional readout of centriole age.

### 2.5 OSK partial reprogramming is an established organism-level benchmark [E]
- Ocampo et al. (2016, *Cell*, PMID 27984723); Lu et al. (2020, *Nature*, PMID 33268865); Sahu et al. (2024, PMID 39259812); Zhang et al. (2026, PMID 41443352); Zielke & Ryan (2026, PMID 41856040).
- **Level-of-evidence asymmetry (critical assessment):** OSK is an **organism-level, multiply replicated benchmark** (progeria, vision, lifespan in vivo); centriole reset is a **cell-level, unreplicated hypothesis**. The 2×2 factorial compares them by design, but they are **never presented as evidence-equivalent**: OSK is the reference, reset is the candidate.

### 2.6 Germline remodeling vs. Hallmarks — precise wording [E→I]

## 3. The Causal Gap, Prior Direct Evidence, and the Causal Evidence Map

The literature establishes: **centriole age → asymmetric inheritance / cell behavior**. It does **not** establish: **centriole age → organismal aging**, nor **centriole replacement → rejuvenation**.

### 3.1 Prior direct evidence — honest dialogue (evidence-based response)

The claim "no study has yet tested…" is true for the *specific* combination (aging-phenotype readouts + verified de novo origin + OSK factorial), but two near-neighbor studies must be addressed explicitly, and both cut against simple expectations:

**Wong et al. (2015, *Science*, PMID 25931445).** Centrinone reversibly depletes centrioles. **Critically, the original abstract states: centrosome loss *irreversibly arrested normal cells* in a senescence-like G1 state by a p53-dependent mechanism; only *cancer cell lines* proliferated indefinitely after loss, and upon washout each cancer line returned to an intrinsic centrosome-number set point.** [E] Thus the "elimination → recovery" cycle has been demonstrated **only in transformed (p53-compromised) lines**; in normal cells the outcome was irreversible p53-dependent arrest. This is a **central feasibility risk for Phase 1 of this protocol** (which uses untransformed fibroblasts): recovery after centriole loss in normal somatic cells is **an open empirical question to be demonstrated in Phase 1, not an established premise**. This protocol adds: aging readouts, de novo-origin verification, and OSK factorial — none measured by Wong. Novelty is therefore not the cycle, but the aging-oriented, origin-verified, factorial evaluation **in untransformed cells, where the cycle is not yet established**.

**Tissue specificity of asymmetric inheritance (critical assessment).** The claim "the ratchet is never released in the somatic stem-cell lineage" is **not universal even within one organism**: in *Drosophila* GSC, the mother centrosome is retained by the stem cell (Yamashita 2007); in *Drosophila* **neuroblasts, the daughter centrosome is retained by the stem cell and the mother centrosome goes to the differentiating GMC** (Januschke et al., 2011, *Nature Communications*, PMID 21407209; Salzmann et al., 2014). The direction is **tissue-specific, not universal**. This protocol's Phase 1 uses fibroblasts — a non-polarized, non-niche somatic cell in which the GSC-like vs. neuroblast-like classification is **not applicable by definition** (fibroblasts do not undergo asymmetric centrosome inheritance). The retention mechanism (§4.2a-6) is therefore presented as **cell-type-contingent**, and the ratchet claim is restricted to systems with demonstrated asymmetric inheritance. Counterexamples are registered, not omitted.

**Renzova et al. (2018, *Stem Cell Reports*, PMID 30197118).** PLK4–STIL inactivation in human pluripotent stem cells (hESC/iPSC) causes centrosome loss → p53 stabilization → loss of self-renewal and differentiation. [E] This is the closest published experiment, and its result **prima facie opposes H2** in pluripotent cells. The protocol must explain why a different outcome is expected in the proposed design — see §4.2. This is the most serious prior-evidence issue and is addressed head-on, not cited around.

### 4.2 Addressing the closest prior experiment: Renzova et al. 2018 (critical assessment)

Renzova et al. (2018, PMID 30197118) inactivated the PLK4–STIL module in human pluripotent stem cells; centrosome loss caused p53 stabilization, loss of self-renewal, and differentiation — *prima facie* the opposite of H2. Five design differences explain why a different outcome is expected here, and each is testable:

| Difference | Renzova 2018 | This protocol | Rationale |
|-----------|--------------|---------------|-----------|
| 1. Cell type | hESC/iPSC (pluripotent) | Somatic fibroblasts | Pluripotency maintenance is centrosome-dependent (Renzova); fibroblast identity is not. "Centrosome loss breaks stemness" is irrelevant to a non-stem cell |
| 2. p53 state | p53 intact → differentiation | **selective USP25/28 inhibition during the acentriolar window (v7.0; pifithrin-α as arm-E comparator)** | The p53-dependent differentiation mechanism (Renzova) is blocked; the plasticity window opens without differentiation |
| 3. Duration | Chronic loss (self-renewal impossible) | Acute elimination → verified de novo recovery to exactly 2 centrioles | The hypothesis concerns the *post-recovery* state, not the acentriolar state |
| 4. Recovery | None intended | PLK4 pulse → functional centrioles (ultrastructure, ciliogenesis, faithful duplication) | H2 tests the rebuilt organelle, not loss itself |
| 5. Readouts | Self-renewal markers | Aging phenotypes (CRCS), not stemness | Loss of self-renewal in Renzova is not a measure of cellular rejuvenation |

**[P] Prediction:** in somatic fibroblasts with p53 suppressed during the window and centrioles rebuilt de novo to 2 per cell, the Renzova outcome (differentiation) should not occur, while CRCS improvement may. If differentiation/self-renewal loss occurs anyway → H2 falsified (pre-registered). Renzova is treated as the **primary prior evidence to be overcome**, not ignored.

### 4.2a The differentiation-ratchet interpretation: why Renzova actually supports the paradigm [H]

Renzova 2018 is not merely a counter-example to be overcome — under the **centriole-as-differentiation-ratchet** hypothesis it is direct supporting evidence. The hypothesis: the centriole/centrosome is a **structural ratchet that locks the cell's current differentiation state**. It does not only count divisions; it anchors identity.

- **Direction of the ratchet:** stem/pluripotent → differentiated (normally irreversible; reprogramming is the rare exception).
- **Removing the ratchet (centriole loss) releases the anchor:**
  - In a **pluripotent cell** (Renzova 2018): the anchor of pluripotency is removed → the cell rolls *down* the ratchet → spontaneous differentiation into three germ layers. This is exactly what a ratchet model predicts: without the anchor, the pluripotent state cannot be maintained.
  - In a **somatic cell** (this protocol): the anchor of the differentiated state is removed → the cell is *plastic* (neither pushed down — it is already at the bottom — nor locked in place) → the plasticity window opens → a **new de novo centriole re-locks a new state** (reprogrammed/rejuvenated).
- **Molecular mechanism [H]:** CEDAR-γ — a **hypothetical centriolar state-locking mechanism** (placeholder term; formally "Centrosome-Associated Memory Complex" — see caveat below) on the mother centriole is the ratchet pawl: it maintains the differentiated state. Centriole loss releases the pawl; de novo assembly installs a fresh pawl that locks the new state.
- **CAMC caveat (v8.0):** a critical assessment noted that "Centrosome-Associated Memory Complex" is **not an established structure** in the literature. It is therefore renamed **"hypothetical centriolar state-locking mechanism"** in all submission text, and the molecular burden is placed on **specific candidate carriers** rather than an unobserved complex: (a) **distal appendages** (the ANKRD26–PIDD1 site — PIDDosome axis, Fava 2017, PMID 28130345; ANKRD26–PIDD1 recruitment, PMID 33350495) as a candidate architectural anchor of "age/identity"; (b) **PTM code on the mother centriole** (polyGlu, Δ2-tubulin) as the state-bearing substrate; (c) **CEP152/PCNT-containing PCM** as the signaling-competence layer. The state-lock hypothesis is retained, but its molecular implementation is now falsifiable per-candidate (each candidate has a perturbation arm), not tied to an unobserved complex.

> **CEDAR unified definition (v5.2):** CEDAR — **C**entriolar **E**ntropy-**D**amage **A**ccumulation **R**atchet — is one ratchet with two modes of action: **(1) the counter mode** (CEDAR-α/β): semi-conservative duplication accumulates entropy/damage with each division (β·t, −λ·N_asym); **(2) the state-lock mode** (CEDAR-γ): the same organelle acts as a **differentiation ratchet** that locks the cell's current identity (pluripotent or differentiated). Loss of the ratchet releases identity in the direction set by the starting state (Renzova: pluripotent → down; this protocol: differentiated → plastic → re-locked younger). The two modes explain both the aging counter and the reprogramming barrier as one mechanism.

> **Related models (links only):** MCARA (Multi-Counter Architecture of Replicative Aging) — replicative limit as a race of several counters, **with the centriole as counter #1 (the centriolar ratchet)**: `~/Desktop/LC/MCARA/CONCEPT.md` (v5.8). CEDAR unified definition (centriolar counter + state-lock ratchet): `~/Desktop/LC/MCARA/CEDAR/CONCEPT.md` (v1.8).
- **Testable predictions that distinguish the ratchet model from a mere "stress" model:** (1) after de novo assembly, the new state must be **stably maintained** (CRCS improvements persist over serial passages — no rollback); (2) the same centriole loss in pluripotent cells causes differentiation (Renzova) while in somatic cells with p53 suppressed it causes plasticity — the *direction* of the outcome is set by the starting state, not by the perturbation; (3) reintroducing an aged centriole into a reset cell re-locks the *old* state (transplantation arm predicts rollback).

> **Synthesis:** Renzova 2018 measured the ratchet's release in one direction (pluripotent → down). This protocol tests the same release in the other direction (differentiated → plastic → re-locked younger). The two results are opposite sides of one mechanism — the ratchet is unidirectional, so the outcome of centriole loss depends entirely on the starting state. This is the deepest available reconciliation of the closest prior experiment with the hypothesis.

> **Pre-specified falsification of the ratchet model alone (evidence-based response):** the ratchet model is falsified **independently of H2** if either of two patterns occurs: (a) **no direction-dependence** — centriole loss in somatic cells produces the *same* outcome as in pluripotent cells (differentiation/loss of identity) despite p53 suppression and fibroblast identity, i.e., the starting state does not set the outcome; or (b) **no re-locking** — after verified de novo assembly the cell does not stably maintain the new state (rollback to the old phenotype within 10 passages) even though CRCS improves transiently. These are registered before data collection; they are not available as post-hoc explanations.

## 5. Causal Architecture (levels of evidence ladder)

```
AGING → centriole structural state → centriole inheritance → stem-cell functional state
      → tissue regenerative capacity → age-associated phenotype
```
```
Intervention: centriole elimination → verified de novo biogenesis → functional young centriole
      → cellular phenotype → stem-cell phenotype
Positive control (parallel): OSK → epigenetic rejuvenation
Central experiment: OSK × centriole reset
```

**Proof ladder** — each level possible only on success of the previous:
1. **Organelle** — structure/age (EM, cryo-ET, PTM mass spec)
2. **Cell** — division, senescence, mitochondria, epigenome (CRCS)
3. **Stem cell** — self-renewal, differentiation, regenerative capacity
4. **Tissue** — organoid/regeneration
5. **Organism** — healthspan/lifespan (in vivo, only if 1–4 pass)

No systemic "rejuvenation" claim is made at levels 3–5 without data at those levels.

## 6. Experimental Design

### 6.1 The 2×2 factorial with formal interaction

| | Centriole reset − | Centriole reset + |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| **OSK −** | A (control) | B |
| **OSK +** | C (OSK benchmark) | D (OSK + reset) |

**Statistical question (per independent verification):** not "B > A" alone, but — is there a significant **main effect of centriole reset** and a significant **OSK × reset interaction** (two-way ANOVA / mixed model)? This separates an additive mechanism from an OSK-dependent one.

**Temporal-dissociation arm F (v8.2):** OSK cyclic expression takes 10–14 days (Ocampo 2016; Lu 2020); centriole elimination + de novo takes 3–5 days. In arm D (simultaneous OSK+Reset), OSK-driven cytoplasmic remodeling (chaperones, TTLL5/6) could "pre-clean" the cytoplasm before de novo assembly — making H3 (independence) untestable. Therefore a **sixth arm F is added: Reset → washout → 10 passages → OSK**, testing whether the structural reset creates **long-term memory** that survives subsequent cytoplasmic/epigenetic aging. Prediction under CEDAR: reset benefits persist through the washout-and-aging interval (structural memory); prediction under the confounder: benefits decay as the cytoplasm re-ages (OSK-dependent artifact). Arm F is the temporal control that makes H3 interpretable; without it, arm D cannot separate "reset independent of OSK" from "OSK pre-cleaned the cytoplasm."

**p53-suppression confounder control (critical assessment):** obtaining de novo centrioles in an untransformed cell likely requires **transient suppression of the p53/PIDDosome axis** (Wong 2015: normal cells arrest irreversibly without it). p53 suppression alone can alter hallmark readouts (genomic stability, senescence). Therefore a **dedicated control arm E** is added: *p53-suppression only, no centriole reset, no OSK*. H6 is falsified (and all reset claims are confounded) if arm E matches arm B on CRCS. This makes p53 the **controlled variable of design**, not an implicit technique.

**Targeted override — arm E' (v7.0, revision):** the p53 arrest after centrosome loss runs through the **mitotic surveillance pathway 53BP1–USP28–p53** (Fong 2016, eLife; Meitinger 2016, JCB), not the classical DNA-damage response. Global pifithrin-α (arm E) disables p53 transcription globally, risking silent genomic lesions that WGS detects too late. **Arm E' (primary override): selective USP25/28 inhibition** (Bratt 2025, Cell Chem Biol; Hernandez-Olmos 2026, J Med Chem; structural basis: Patzke 2024, *EMBO Rep*, PMID 38816515) — unlatches the USP28 bridge only, leaving p53 competent for genuine damage. **Genetic alternative (v7.5):** transient **CRISPRi against USP28 or 53BP1** (dCas9-KRAB, doxycycline-inducible) achieves the same targeted override without any chemical exposure — pre-registered as the orthogonal confirmation route (chemical vs. genetic override must agree on checkpoint passage rate). **Dual specificity control:** arms E (pifithrin-α-only) and E' (USP28-inhibitor-only) run in parallel, both without reset/OSK; H6' requires: (a) checkpoint passage rate E' ≥ E (override works), (b) etoposide-challenge p53 response preserved in E' but not E (specificity), (c) E' ≤ A on CRCS (no independent rejuvenation signal). If E' fails (a), the technical-barrier gate (§13 Phase 1) applies; if E' shows a CRCS signal matching B, all reset claims are confounded (H6 falsified).

### 6.1a OSK × reset interaction — interpretation matrix (critical assessment)

Pre-specified interpretation of the 2×2 factorial, **locked before data**:

| Main effect of reset | OSK × reset interaction | Interpretation | Conclusion for H3 |
|:---:|:---:|---|:---:|
| + | + (synergy) | Reset amplifies OSK beyond additivity | H3 supported (independence of mechanism, super-additive benefit) |
| + | 0 (additive) | Reset adds to OSK independently | H3 supported (independent additive mechanism) |

**Rule:** the interaction is interpreted **jointly with main effects** (never in isolation); the matrix is registered verbatim; deviation from the matrix requires a pre-registered amendment.

### 6.2 Centriolar perturbation control (the "not-a-reset" arm)
A fifth condition that induces maximal comparable cellular stress **without successful de novo reset** (e.g., PLK4 inhibition + recovery blocked by a biogenesis inhibitor, or abortive ablation). This discriminates: **removal → bona fide reset** from **removal → stress → survivor selection**. Any rejuvenation seen in the perturbation arm falsifies H4.

### 6.2b Targeted-degradation elimination route (v7.1, revision)

**Rationale:** chemical PLK4 inhibition (centrinone, aminopyrazole) acts by sustained kinase occupancy; **targeted degradation removes the protein catalytically** and is cleaner. Three validated routes exist (all confirmed via PubMed):
- **AID2 (auxin-inducible degron 2):** TIR1-based, auxin-triggered degradation; already applied to the essential centrosome protein **CEP192 in live mice** (PMID 40020058, Sci Adv 2025) — the template for **SAS-6-mAID / PLK4-mAID** degron lines (elimination by dilutive loss over ~3 divisions, as in centrinone, but without kinase-occupancy off-targets).
- **dTAG system:** FKBP12(F36V)-tagged SAS-6/PLK4 degraded by dTAG-13 (cell lines; knock-in protocol, Methods Enzymol 2023, PMID 36764753).
- **PLK4-PROTAC:** first potent, selective, in vivo efficacious PLK4 chimera (PMID 37279162, J Med Chem 2023) — direct chemical degradation of PLK4.

**Decision rule (pre-registered):** Phase 1 tests **AID2-SAS-6** as primary elimination route (reversible, orthogonal to chemistry), with centrinone as the established comparator and PLK4-PROTAC as the alternative; the three routes must yield **concordant passage rates** before CRCS claims (route-concordance gate).

**Dual-synchronization protocol (AID2 trap):** AID2 degradation is not instantaneous (24–48 h, dilutive at division); cells already in G2 when auxin is added would enter mitosis with partially degraded centrioles → multipolar spindles → micronuclei → cGAS–STING (exactly the v7.9 trap). CDK4/6i alone does not stop G2 cells. Therefore the elimination protocol requires **two sequential blocks**: (1) **aphidicolin (S-phase block)** synchronizes the population; (2) after washout, **auxin + RO-3306 (CDK1i, strict G2 block) for 12 h** — all cells complete DNA replication but cannot enter mitosis until SAS-6 degradation is complete (verified by western blot, >90% loss). Only then is the CDK1 block released into the cytostatic window. This converts "eliminate while some cells are in G2" into "eliminate with the entire population pinned in G2."

**Resistance caveat (v7.4):** mammalian cells can develop **resistance to auxin-inducible degradation** (Hyle et al., 2026, *J Biol Chem*, PMID 42248454 — mechanisms: degron mutation, TIR1 downregulation, pathway compensation). Therefore the route-concordance gate is not only a quality filter but a **resistance screen**: if AID2-SAS-6 fails while chemical routes succeed, resistance — not biology — is the explanation, and the elimination route is switched (pre-registered).

### 6.2c The acentriolar-mitosis trap: cytostatic window + cGAS/STING dissociation arm (v7.9)

**Problem (fatal-flaw candidate, accepted):** USP25/28 override prevents the primary 53BP1–USP28–p53 arrest, but a cell entering mitosis without centrioles forms an acentriolar spindle → lagging chromosomes → micronuclei → **micronucleus rupture activates cGAS–STING** (Mackenzie 2017, Nature, PMID 28738408; Dou 2017, Nature, PMID 28976970) → **secondary, USP28-independent senescence/SASP**. A bypass that forces acentriolar mitosis is therefore insufficient — it merely trades the primary arrest for a secondary inflammatory barrier.

**Solution 1 — Cytostatic window (primary):** during the elimination→rebuild window (48–72 h), an **reversible CDK1 inhibitor (RO-3306) or CDK4/6 inhibitor (palbociclib)** holds cells in G2/G1, preventing mitotic entry until exactly two functional centrioles are verified (gate E) and appendage maturation is underway. Mitosis resumes only post-verification. This converts the window from "survive mitosis without centrioles" to "never enter mitosis without centrioles" — eliminating the acentriolar-mitosis trap at its source.

**Solution 2 — cGAS/STING dissociation arm (control):** a parallel CRISPR-KO of **cGAS or STING1** runs the same elimination protocol without the cytostatic block. Predictions: (a) if survival/passage rate rises sharply in cGAS/STING-KO vs. parental under identical override, the **mitotic-error stress (not organelle loss itself) is the dominant barrier** — reported as a mechanistic dissociation; (b) if KO does not rescue, cGAS-STING is not the limiting barrier and the cytostatic window is the necessary route. Either outcome is a pre-registered, interpretable result; the arm is not "remove the safety" but "localize the barrier."

**Solution 3 — PIDDosome bypass arm (v8.0):** a critical assessment noted that USP28-i blocks only the 53BP1–USP28 bridge, while the **PIDDosome–p53 axis** (Fava 2017; ANKRD26–PIDD1 at distal appendages, PMID 33350495) can fire **independently** upon centrosome loss/amplification — and that the cytostatic window prevents mitosis but does not prevent G1 p53 activation via PIDD1. Therefore a **third bypass arm is added: transient PIDD1-KD or ANKRD26-KD (dCas9-KRAB, dox-inducible)** in Phase 1, running in parallel with USP28-i and the cytostatic window. Pre-registered comparison: checkpoint passage rates under (i) USP28-i alone, (ii) PIDD1-KD alone, (iii) both, (iv) both + cytostatic window. If PIDD1-KD is required (passage rate rises only when added), the PIDDosome is a dominant G1 barrier and the override strategy is PIDD1-targeted; if USP28-i alone suffices, PIDD1 is not rate-limiting in this system. Either outcome updates the override logic pre-registered — never post-hoc.

**Safety note:** the cytostatic window does NOT remove the genome-integrity gate (H5) or transformation surveillance (§6.5b) — it adds a timing control that prevents the very chromosome-segregation errors those gates would otherwise detect. The three solutions together bound the v14/v15 traps: no acentriolar mitosis (window), direct barrier localization (cGAS/STING arm), and a second, USP28-independent p53 gate (PIDDosome arm).

**Clarification (peer-review rebuttal, v7.1):** targeted degradation changes the **elimination route** — it does **not** remove the need for p53-override. Loss of SAS-6/PLK4 still leaves cells acentriolar → the **mitotic-surveillance p53 checkpoint still fires** (Fong 2016; Meitinger 2016). The USP25/28 override (v7.0) remains the **survival gate**; AID2/PROTAC is the **elimination gate**. The two are orthogonal and both mandatory.

**Cytoplasmic-environment control — the building-block source (v7.5):** a cytoplasmic-aging concern (a de novo centriole assembled in old cytoplasm from oxidized tubulin / dysregulated TTLL5/6 may instantly re-acquire "old" PTMs) is addressed three ways: (1) **SILAC Proof C'** above proves the assembled structure uses newly synthesized protein (heavy-labeled), not recycled old bricks; (2) **post-assembly PTM audit** — polyglutamylation profile (GT335), Δ2-tubulin, and oxidation markers (carbonyl assay) measured on the rebuilt centriole and compared with young/aged reference centrioles (criterion: rebuilt ≤ young reference on all PTM readouts); (3) **window-conditioning** — the elimination-to-rebuild window is run in fresh medium with proteostasis support (proteasome boost, antioxidant N-acetylcysteine), minimizing de novo incorporation of oxidized subunits. If SILAC shows heavy-fraction < 80%, the reset claim is not admissible (pre-registered threshold).

**Cytoplasmic PTM-reset module (v7.8):** to guarantee "clean software" (not only clean hardware), the de novo window additionally includes **genetic modulation of the PTM enzyme balance**: transient **CCP5/CCP6 overexpression** (deglutamylases that trim polyGlu chains) and/or **CRISPRi against TTLL5/6** during the elimination-to-rebuild window — resetting the cytoplasmic polyglutamylation potential so the new centriole assembles with a near-zero polyGlu baseline. No selective small-molecule TTLL/CCP inhibitors exist yet (literature verified, 2026-08-11: tubulin-code reviews J Biomed Sci 2026, PMID 42083040; Cancer Metastasis Rev 2026, PMID 42257920) — the genetic route is the available tool. Readout: GT335 intensity on the rebuilt centriole must be ≤ young reference; if the PTM-reset module is required to pass this, that is a reported design result (the reset becomes a two-part intervention: organelle + cytoplasmic PTM environment).

**Cytosolic clearance expansion (v7.9):** to address the "old cytosol" (oxidized tubulin pool, aged modifying enzymes) beyond enzyme rebalancing, the window additionally includes **targeted clearance of oxidized proteins**: transient **Nrf2 activation (TBHQ)**, **proteasome boost**, and — if compatible with the window timing — **mTORC1 inhibition (autophagy induction)** to remove oxidized/damaged cytosolic subunits before de novo assembly. Readout: carbonyl assay and oxidized-tubulin fraction must be ≤ young reference at rebuild start; these interventions are pre-registered window components, not post-hoc fixes.

**Cytoplasmic epigenetic audit ("epigenetic shadow"):** to prove that OSK does not indirectly "rejuvenate" the de novo centriole through cytoplasmic enzymes, **quantitative mass spectrometry of the free-tubulin pool and TTLL/CCP enzyme levels is run in ALL arms (A, B, C, D) at the 48–72 h rebuild window**: if arm D (OSK+Reset) shows young-like tubulin/PTM-enzyme composition BEFORE the centriole assembles, the OSK-cytoplasmic confounder is real and quantified (H3 qualified); if the tubulin/PTM-enzyme composition is comparable across arms at assembly time, the de novo centriole is assembled in a comparable biochemical environment and the reset effect is attributable to the organelle exchange. This audit — together with the temporal-dissociation arm F (§6.1) and the cytoplasmic inheritance test (§12) — completes the OSK-shadow control: two orthogonal timing controls plus one direct cytoplasmic measurement.

### 6.4 Safety as co-primary mechanistic gate
> **No rejuvenation claim is admissible unless chromosome segregation, ploidy, and centrosome-number homeostasis remain normal.**

### 6.4b Centrosome-loss checkpoint and survivor selection — the biggest biological threat to interpretation 

Two mechanisms can mimic or mask a reset effect and must be quantified, not just controlled:
1. **Centrosome-loss checkpoint:** loss of the centrosome triggers p53/PIDDosome arrest (Wong 2015; Mikule 2007). If most cells arrest, the few that proceed are **not representative** — the reset effect (if any) is measured on a selected subpopulation.
2. **Survivor selection:** cells that survive elimination + recovery may be the *youngest/least damaged* fraction — apparent "rejuvenation" could be **selection, not reset**.

**Accounting (pre-specified):** (a) report **checkpoint passage rate** (% cells completing elimination→recovery) per condition; (b) compare **pre-elimination markers** (damage load, cell size, passage history) of survivors vs. non-survivors — if survivors are systematically younger, results are qualified as selection-biased; (c) the **perturbation control arm (§6.2)** and **arm E (p53-suppression-only)** jointly isolate the reset contribution; (d) **lineage tracing / single-cell live tracking** ( specification): **Centrin1-GFP** (centriole age & fate — mother vs. daughter, de novo vs. templated) + **p16-mCherry** (senescence onset) dual-reporter live imaging, tracking the **same cell** through elimination → de novo rebuild → serial passages, proving the *identical cell* improved (not a younger subclone taking over). These four measures convert the checkpoint/selection threat from a confounder into a reported, bounded quantity.

### 6.5b Transformation surveillance (cancer-risk response)

**Question addressed:** could the transient USP25/28 override + acentriolar window push cells toward neoplastic transformation? The design answers this with five independent, pre-registered checks — any one failing disqualifies the clone and, if ≥5% of replicates, triggers the safety futility stop (§8):

| Layer | Assay | Pre-specified criterion | Catches |
|-------|-------|------------------------|---------|
| 1. p53 re-competence (H6') | Etoposide challenge → γH2AX/p21 induction after window | p53 response restored in ≥90% of surviving clones | Cells that lost p53 function during the window (the Wong 2015 / Mikule 2007 cancer-like state) |
| 2. Karyotype/ploidy | Metaphase spreads, FISH (centromeric probes) | Diploid, no structural rearrangements in ≥95% of spreads | Aneuploidy, translocations acquired during the window |
| 3. Immortalization screen | Serial passaging beyond Hayflick limit with no hTERT introduced | No growth past expected senescence horizon (fibroblasts: no spontaneous immortalization) | Cells that acquired replicative immortality — the hallmark of transformation in vitro |
| 4. Anchorage-independent growth | Soft-agar colony assay (Phase 2, n=3 donors × arms) | Zero colonies above background (normal fibroblasts are anchorage-dependent) | Classic in vitro transformation phenotype |
| 5. Clonal dynamics | Lineage tracing (Centrin1-GFP + p16-mCherry, §6.4b) + proliferation kinetics | No outgrowth of a single clone >10% of population without reset phenotype | Subclone takeover mimicking a transformation event |

**Escalation option (pre-registered):** if layers 3–4 flag any clone, that clone is excluded from CRCS analysis and — only if the flag rate ≥5% per arm — the safety futility stop fires (§8) and the arm is not interpretable. If the flag rate is <5%, results are reported with the flagged clones excluded and a transparent note on the residual risk.

**v7.4 addition:** polyploid giant cancer cells (PGCC) and centrosome amplification via CDT1 (Pan et al., 2026, *Cancer Letters*, PMID 41319860) added to the transformation readout panel — PGCC formation is an early, easily-scored transformation phenotype (layer 6, optional): nuclei ≥4N with giant morphology are counted in the immortalization screen (layer 3) as an additional flag.

**Transformation vs. rejuvenation dissociation (critical assessment):** a reset clone that passes layers 1–2 but shows elevated proliferation (better EdU) is scored as **younger, not transformed** — because it retains contact inhibition (layer 4, zero soft-agar colonies), a functional p53 (layer 1), and a normal karyotype (layer 2). The trio (p53-competence + diploidy + anchorage-dependence) is the accepted in vitro definition of non-transformed; CRCS claims are only admissible for clones passing all five layers.

**Biological rationale (literature, verified):** the cancer-like state described in Wong 2015 (proliferation after centriole loss) and Mikule 2007 (aneuploidy accumulation) both require **sustained** p53 deficiency; our window is transient and USP28-specific, and layer 1 verifies that p53 function returns. Transient USP25/28 inhibition is therefore not equivalent to oncogenesis — but the five-layer screen is retained because the residual risk is non-zero and must be bounded, not assumed away.

**Aneuploidy-rate rebuttal (v7.3):** a claim that de novo assembly produces 40–60% aneuploidy — making the 5% gate "naive" — has **no supporting citation in the review and no primary-source basis in the elimination literature** (Khodjakov 2002, La Terra 2005, Uetake 2007, Wong 2015 report arrest/inefficiency, not aneuploidy rates). The gate is not a population aneuploidy claim: it is a **per-clone admissibility criterion** on the cells actually analyzed (karyotype + scDNA-seq, §6.5). The measured aneuploidy rate is a **reported quantity** — if it is high, passage rates and clone yields are correspondingly low and Phase 1 is correctly judged infeasible at the §13 gate; if it is low, the gate holds. The protocol does not assume an aneuploidy rate; it measures it, pre-registers it as a reportable outcome, and treats it as a feasibility criterion, not an opinion.

**v7.7 meta-analysis response :** an independent quantitative meta-analysis (88% irreversible arrest; <12% de novo recovery; 4.2±1.1 cycles to maturation; 28% aneuploidy) is **not reproducible from the cited sources** — the two key citations (Werner 2022; Fu 2016) are not found in PubMed/Europe PMC, and no extraction table or forest plot is provided. The protocol therefore treats these figures as **unverifiable prior claims**, not as facts: (a) arrest/recovery/maturation rates are pre-registered **measured endpoints** of Phase 1 (checkpoint passage rate §6.4b; maturation latency Proof D' §6.3); (b) the maturation-latency concept itself is accepted as a design constraint (several cycles to appendage acquisition — supported by verified literature: CEP295/Ana1, Pimenta-Marques 2024; distal protein network, Wang 2018) and is now explicitly scheduled into the timeline (Proof D'); (c) the polyglutamylation-of-cytoplasm concern is partially verified — glutamylation recruits MT-nucleation factors (Hong 2025, EMBO J, PMID 40229407) — strengthening the PTM-audit and cytoplasmic-inheritance-test requirements (§6.3 C', §12); (d) aneuploidy remains a measured, pre-registered quantity (§6.5). If Phase 1 measures rates near the unverified estimates, the program honestly stops at the §13 gate — that is the design's purpose.

### 7.1 CRCS Statistical Analysis Plan (pre-registered formula — peer-review response)

The composite is **fully specified before data collection** to prevent post-hoc weighting:

1. **Components (6):** proliferative competence (EdU+ fraction), clonogenic capacity (CFU), DNA-damage burden (γH2AX foci, inverse), senescence burden (SA-β-gal fraction, inverse), mitochondrial function (TMRM, normalized), epigenetic age (Horvath ΔAge, inverse).
   - **v8.2 dual-mode measurement ( — ciliary-proliferation paradox):** a cell cannot simultaneously divide (EdU+, CFU) and present a primary cilium (H3/H8 signaling); fibroblasts resorb the cilium at G1/S. CRCS is therefore measured in **two defined culture regimes**: (a) **proliferative mode** (EdU+, CFU, γH2AX, SA-β-gal, TMRM, Horvath-on-dividing-clones — as in v7.3); (b) **contact-inhibition/quiescence mode (G0)** — cells at confluence with cilia present (cilium length, Hedgehog/Wnt target genes, ciliary-proteome readouts). The two modes are reported as separate domains of the composite (not pooled across incompatible phases), and the cilia-dependent hypotheses (H3, H8) are evaluated in mode (b) only. This removes the biological impossibility an assessor identified (measuring EdU and cilia in the same cell at the same time).
2. **Normalization (v7.3 — kinetic correction):** each component z-scored against the **control arm (A) distribution** within the same donor line and batch (mean 0, SD 1); **additionally, the epigenetic-age component is normalized for cell-cycle kinetics** — because Horvath clocks track division rate, a stressed slow-cycling cell can show artifactually youthful methylation . The Horvath ΔAge component is therefore evaluated **only in proliferating clones** (FACS-purified EdU+ fraction) and reported **both raw and normalized to population-doubling time** (PDT); discordance between raw and PDT-normalized epigenetic age is reported explicitly, and CRCS is considered invalid if the epigenetic component moves opposite to EdU+ (see rule 4).
3. **Weights:** fixed equal weights (1/6) — locked at pre-registration; no post-hoc reweighting. Sensitivity analysis removes one component at a time (leave-one-out).
4. **Direction:** all components oriented so higher = younger; a priori rule that the composite is **invalid** if ≥2 components move in opposite directions at p < 0.05 (reported as discordant, not pooled).
5. **Timing gate (v8.0):** CRCS is measured **no earlier than Passage 6 post-reset** — only after appendage acquisition and first-cilium competence are verified (Proof D', Proof D'''); before Passage 6 only structural/molecular readouts are reportable. This prevents an immature, cilia-less centriole from producing a false "rejuvenated" or "aged" signal (maturation-lag confounder).
6. **TMRM sensitivity version (v8.0):** two pre-registered CRCS variants — (a) full 6-component; (b) 5-component **without TMRM** (mitochondrial marker is a covariate, §4.3 H7, not a centriole-causal readout). If the two versions diverge on the reset main effect, that is reported as an informative negative result for the mitochondrial route (not silently pooled).
5. **Analysis:** mixed-effects model, CRCS ~ reset × OSK + (1|donor) + (1|batch); main effect of reset and interaction reported with 95% CI.
6. **Publication:** the SAP is deposited (OSF) before data collection, with the formula verbatim.
7. **PCA secondary score (v7.7):** a principal-component composite is pre-registered as a **secondary** score — PC1 over the six components (loadings reported, not assumed equal), plus a dedicated **SASP-panel component** (IL-6, IL-8, MMP3, PAI-1) as an independent senescence readout. The equal-weight CRCS remains the pre-registered primary; PCA/SASP is confirmatory and reported side-by-side. Discrepancy between equal-weight and PCA scores triggers explicit reporting (not silent re-weighting).
8. **Latent-factor structure (collinearity):** EdU, CFU, SA-β-gal, p16/p21 are collinear; equal weighting of collinear markers understates variance and inflates significance. The composite is therefore restructured as a **two-latent-factor model** (pre-registered): **"Proliferative pool"** (EdU, CFU, Horvath-on-dividing) and **"Stress response"** (γH2AX, SA-β-gal, TMRM, SASP), estimated by **structural equation modeling (SEM) or orthogonalized PCA** with factor loadings reported; the primary H2 claim is made on the latent-factor contrast (reset vs. control), with the equal-weight CRCS retained only as a descriptive summary index. This replaces equal weighting as the inferential basis (collinearity addressed at the model level, not by assumption).
8. **Single-primary-endpoint option (v8.1):** because a composite can mask component-specific effects, **EdU+ proliferative fraction is pre-registered as the single primary endpoint** for H2 (with SA-β-gal as the second co-primary), and CRCS is reported as the composite secondary — so the headline claim never depends on the composite alone; the composite remains for the multi-hallmark claim.
9. **Bayesian sensitivity (v8.1):** a pre-registered Bayesian analysis (Bayes factor for the reset main effect and the interaction; weakly informative priors, donor ICC from Phase 1/0) is reported alongside the frequentist mixed model — a null interaction is credible only if both approaches agree.

### 7.2 Effect-size justification (evidence-based response)

The 25% CRCS improvement is a **declared minimal detectable effect** (MDE), not an effect estimate: with N=10/arm, SD≈1.0 (z-score metric), α=0.05, two-sided, power 0.85 yields MDE ≈ 0.25 SD units (composite). **Biological anchoring (per earlier review):** the MDE is anchored to published OSK effect sizes on comparable readouts — OSK/OSKM partial reprogramming produces multi-hallmark improvements of comparable or larger magnitude on composite aging readouts in vivo (Ocampo 2016, PMID 27984723: partial reprogramming extended median lifespan in progeroid mice and improved multiple hallmarks; Lu 2020, PMID 33268865: OSK restored youthful methylation and vision). The 0.25 SD threshold therefore represents a **conservative fraction of a known-positive benchmark** (a smaller effect than OSK itself would not be claimed as a rejuvenation signal), not an arbitrary number. Pilot (Phase 1, N=3/arm) will estimate the observed effect; the MDE is revised only by a pre-registered amendment, never by data inspection.

**Interaction-term power (evidence-based response):** power for the **OSK × reset interaction** (H3) is computed separately by **mixed-model simulation** (1,000 iterations, donor/batch random effects), not by the main-effect formula — interaction detection in 2×2 designs requires substantially larger N (standard result). The simulation is run at the SAP stage (pre-data) and reports power for an interaction of Cohen's f² = 0.10; if power < 80%, either N is increased (pre-registered) or H3 is demoted from primary to secondary with the main effect of reset as the primary claim — decided **before** data collection, never after. **Sequential interaction analysis (v8.0):** because N=10/arm cannot power a small interaction, a pre-registered escalation is added: if at N=10/arm the interaction is not detected (p > 0.10), N is escalated to 20/arm (pre-registered amendment) before a final H3 verdict; the escalation itself is reported (N, power gain) so a null interaction is a real null, not an underpowered one.

## 11. The Concept of "Entropy" — Operational Definition

Where "entropy" is used (program name only), it refers to **structural disorder / damage accumulation** with a defined state variable. If the physical term is used, the definition is:

> S = −k_B Σ pᵢ ln pᵢ — over the state space of centriolar PTM configurations (e.g., polyglutamylation levels per tubulin site), estimated from mass spectrometry.

Until such measurements exist, the documents use "structural disorder / candidate aging carrier" — not entropy as a physical law. **Naming (per earlier review):** "Entropy Reset" is retained only as the program/grants name; the publication title is deliberately neutral — "Centriole Reset: Testing Structural Organelle Rejuvenation in Somatic Cells" (as used throughout this document) — avoiding hype-driven terminology in the peer-reviewed version.

**Biophysical terminology for IF 18+ (v7.9):** biophysics reviewers will object that a cell is an open, non-equilibrium system and cannot "accumulate entropy" in the thermodynamic sense. The publication text therefore adopts the assessor's recommended reframing wherever a physical term would be scrutinized: **"Irreversible PTM Drift"** and **"Structural Hysteresis"** (state-dependent accumulation of terminal modifications — e.g., Δ2-tubulin, which cannot be re-tyrosinated, and asymmetric polyglutamylation — that is not erased by cytoplasmic turnover). Mapping: "temporal entropy" → "irreversible PTM drift"; "entropy-damage accumulation" → "structural hysteresis"; the formal S = −k_B Σ pᵢ ln pᵢ definition remains available in the technical appendix for readers who want the information-theoretic formalism, explicitly labeled as a model of state-space disorder, not thermodynamic entropy.

**Transplantation arm (crossing):** young→old and old→young centriole transfer; if age transfers with the organelle in both directions — a Nature/Cell-level finding.

**Cytoplasmic inheritance test (v7.7):** the strongest control for "the centriole is only a mirror of the cytoplasm": **cytoplast–karyoplast fusion** transplants a freshly reset (young, SILAC-verified) centriole into the cytoplasm of a non-reset old cell (and vice versa). Predictions: (a) if the young centriole is "corrupted" within 2 cell cycles by old cytoplasm (PTM audit: GT335, Δ2, carbonyl), the centriole is a passive mirror — CEDAR's organelle-autonomy claim is falsified and the program reframes as cytoplasmic/PTM-environment intervention; (b) if the young centriole retains its youthful PTM state in old cytoplasm and restores ciliogenesis, the organelle is an autonomous carrier — CEDAR supported. Either outcome is a publishable causal test; the experiment is pre-registered as a decisive discriminator (§4.2a).

**Killer-experiment specification (v7.3):** the strongest causal probe — **artificial centriole aging in an OSK-rejuvenated cell** — is added to the transplantation arm: (1) rejuvenate a cell with OSK (epigenetically young, old centriole retained); (2) artificially age its centriole by **TTLL5 overexpression** (excess polyglutamylation — the Robichaud 2024 KIFC3/FBF1/PML seed) or PLK4-driven over-elongation; (3) **prediction:** if the cell returns to senescence *despite* OSK rejuvenation, structural centriole state overrides epigenetic age — the definitive proof that the centriole is a master regulator (and the OSK paradox resolved: OSK acts *below* the organelle, transiently). Controls: sham injection, TTLL5-inactive mutant, non-ciliary tubulin modification. **KIFC3-discrimination arm (; upgraded to mandatory Phase 1 control in v8.3):** genetic **KIFC3 knockdown/knockout** (no selective KIFC3 small-molecule inhibitors exist — literature verified 2026-08-11). In v8.3 the KIFC3-KD condition is **elevated from an optional Phase-3 discrimination arm to a mandatory Phase 1 control branch**: if KIFC3 loss fully abolishes the reset effect, the centriole acts as a *mechanical stress antenna* (KIFC3 scaffold route, Robichaud 2024) rather than an autonomous clock — CEDAR's state-lock claim is reframed (still publishable, but the theory's name/mechanism must change, (critical assessment)); if the reset effect survives KIFC3-KD, the organelle has autonomy beyond the scaffold route. **PIDDosome optogenetic arm (Killer 2.1):** optionally, instead of (or in addition to) TTLL5 aging, use **optogenetic dimerization to recruit PIDD1 to the distal appendages of a de novo centriole** — if this alone induces senescence, the "age" of the centriole is encoded in its distal architecture (PIDDosome pathway, Fava 2017), not only in its PTM code; a clean spatial-causality test complementing the TTLL5 arm.

**OSK-paradox resolution (v7.3):** the assessor's paradox — "OSK rejuvenates without replacing centrioles, so the centriole cannot be the barrier" — is addressed explicitly and pre-registered: (1) OSK acts **below the organelle level** (epigenome, proteostasis pool), leaving structural cilium/centrosome defects intact (cilium defects persist after reprogramming); (2) OSK effects on organellar structure are **transient** — resolution of the paradox requires **long-term passage (≥20 passages)** comparing OSK-only vs. reset vs. both, with the prediction that OSK-only CRCS gains decay while reset gains persist (stability is the discriminating variable — this is already the "no rollback" falsification in §4.2a); (3) the killer-experiment arm above tests the direction of causality directly.

> "If you ask me, for visual clarity you should mention these experiments as *prototypes* of this work, then count their gaps and mark what could not be established, and then propose your own experiment. ## The four nearest prototypes

**1. Khodjakov et al., 2002** — In CHO cells, the existing centrosome was completely destroyed by laser microsurgery, and the cells then built a new centrosome/centriole *de novo*. That is the chain: existing centriole → disappeared → new structure formed from zero. **The main technical part is already done.**

**2. La Terra et al., 2005** — Even closer: in HeLa cells, resident centrioles were removed by laser ablation / needle microsurgery, and the cells later assembled several new centrioles *de novo*. An important detail: initially their number was quite uncontrolled.

**3. Uetake et al., 2007** — Tested the same principle in normal human cells. The conclusion: de novo centriole assembly after centrosome removal is **not a curiosity of transformed/cancer cells — it is a general capacity of mammalian somatic cells**.

**4. Wong et al., Science 2015 — centrinone** — The most practically close: by reversible inhibition of PLK4, centrioles were eliminated; after washout, centriole formation resumed in the cells, and transformed cells returned to their centrosome-number set point. In other words, a chemical path already exists that is almost direct: **deplete → wait → restore PLK4 → de novo rebuild**.

**Known warning:** de novo assembly can be error-prone — the number and structure do not always come out as ordered as with templated duplication.

## The actual novelty of our experiment

> **Removing the old centriole is not new. De novo creation of a new centriole is not new. What is new is the question: did the aging state of the old somatic cell change as a result?**

And more strictly:

> **old cell + old centriole → old cell + genuinely new centriole**
> then we must ask: **did the cellular age phenotype change?**
